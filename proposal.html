ES350 Camera ADAS

A Raspberry Pi camera system for a Lexus ES350 that shows the blind spot on a dash display, then learns to detect what is in it.

Vehicle Lexus ES350
Compute Pi 5 + Hailo-8L
Display 10.1" · 1280×800
Phases 3
Project goal

See the adjacent lane without turning your head

Put a 10-inch display in the cabin and a camera on each side of the car. When a turn signal comes on, the display shows that side's camera. From there, the same camera frames feed an on-board ML model that detects vehicles in the immediately adjacent lane on the freeway and flags them with low latency. A forward camera later extends the system to collision warning.

The Pi is in the car from Phase 1, so the cameras, display pipeline, and recorded footage all carry forward into the ML phases.

  1. P1
    Side view on turn signal
    Indicator on shows that side's camera on the display.
  2. P2
    Blind spot detection
    Alert on the display when a vehicle is in the adjacent lane.
  3. P3
    Forward collision detection
    Warn when the vehicle ahead is closing too fast.
Architecture

One camera pipeline, three layers on top

Every phase reads the same captured frames. Phase 1 routes them to the display. Phase 2 adds a detection branch that sends an alert back to the compositor. Phase 3 adds a forward camera and a time-to-collision estimate.

Phase 1 · side view and recording Phase 2 · blind spot detection Phase 3 · forward collision
P1Frame captureReads both side cameras continuously, whether or not the display is showing them.
P1Blinker listenerReads the turn-signal state and tells the compositor which side to show.
P1CompositorShows the selected feed full-screen and draws alerts on top. Idle when no indicator is on.
P1RecorderSaves raw clips to the SSD to build the training and evaluation dataset.
P2Detector + trackerDetects vehicles on the Hailo, tracks them across frames, and keeps only those in the adjacent lane.
P3TTC estimatorTracks the vehicle ahead, estimates distance and closing speed, and derives time-to-collision.

Display requirements

  • 1000+ nits so it stays readable in direct sun.
  • 1280×800 resolution.
  • 12V input so it runs straight from car power.
  • No logo or "No Signal" screen at boot.

Mounting

  • Location: lower center, below the factory screen, angled toward the driver. Adjustable, no drilling. Check that it doesn't cover the climate controls.
  • Fixing: bolted through the VESA holes. No adhesive, vent, or suction mounts, since the panel weighs about 3 lb.
  • Keep clear of the windshield view and airbag zones (passenger dash, knee area).
  • Cables run under the trim panels.
Project phases

Three phases, each building on the last

01

Side view on turn signal

No ML. Hardware, wiring, and the display pipeline.

Turn on the left indicator and the left camera appears on the display. Same for the right. No need to look over your shoulder.

  • Install both side cameras and the display.
  • Tap the turn-signal state into the Pi.
  • Stream the selected side to the display with minimal latency.
  • Record drives to the SSD from day one to build the Phase 2 dataset.
  • Drive with the setup for a couple of weeks before permanent mounting.
Done whenThe correct side feed appears quickly and reliably whenever the indicator is on, in daylight and at night.
02

Blind spot detection

The core ML problem. Freeway driving, adjacent lane only.

Show an alert, such as a red dot, on the display when a vehicle is in the adjacent lane.

  • Only vehicles in the immediately adjacent lane. Ignore vehicles two lanes over, oncoming traffic, and parked objects.
  • Run a vehicle detector on the Hailo using the side camera frames.
  • Track objects across frames and filter to the adjacent lane, starting with a region of interest and moving to a learned lane position.
  • Draw the alert on the side view, and alert when the indicator is on toward an occupied lane.
  • Label recorded footage and fine-tune or train the model for this camera angle.
  • Priorities: low latency, few false alerts, no missed vehicles in the lane.
Done whenAlerts match real blind-spot occupancy on recorded and live freeway drives, with acceptable latency and false-alert rate.
03

Forward collision detection

Adds a forward camera and a time-to-collision estimate.

Warn the driver when the vehicle ahead is closing too fast.

  • Add a forward-facing camera.
  • Detect vehicles in the ego lane and track them over time.
  • Estimate distance and closing speed, and derive time-to-collision.
  • Show a visual warning when time-to-collision drops below a threshold.
Done whenWarnings trigger reliably when closing on a vehicle, without frequent false alarms in normal following traffic.
